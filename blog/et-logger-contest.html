2026-10-18 Field Logger · Contesting

Contest logging in the Field Logger — CQ WW scoring, live

The LiaisonOS Field Logger started life as a POTA logger. In 2.4.6 it grows an optional contest layer — a CQ-zone picker, a per-band duplicate guard, a live scoreboard that climbs as you work the band, and one-click Cabrillo export — without giving up anything that made it good for parks. A session can be POTA, contest, or both.

Sylvain Deguire (VA2OPS) · LiaisonOS 2.4.6

Field Logger · CQ WW DX · Zone picker + live score + Cabrillo export

A contest layer, not a different app

Field day, a CQ WW weekend, or a POTA activation that turns into a mini-pile-up — they all want slightly different things from a logger, and most people end up juggling two programs. 2.4.6 keeps it in one: when you start a session you can now pick a Contest, and the logger grows exactly the fields that contest needs — nothing more. Leave it on None and it's the same POTA logger you already know.

The contest choices are CQ WW DX (SSB, CW or RTTY), ARRL Field Day, or a custom name. The one you pick becomes the CONTEST: line in the Cabrillo export later, and for CQ WW it switches on a set of fast-entry helpers built around the one thing that contest trades: the CQ zone.

POTA and contest at once. A park activation during a contest weekend is common, so the two layers aren't exclusive. Your POTA park fields stay exactly where they were; the contest fields appear alongside them. One QSO feeds both the ADIF (POTA) and the Cabrillo (contest) export.

Fast entry for CQ WW

In a pile-up the enemy is keystrokes. The CQ WW helpers are built to keep both the exchange and the dupe check out of your way:

🔢
Zone picker (01–39)
The received exchange in CQ WW is the other station's CQ zone. A big-button picker sits right next to RST — tap the zone, done. Single digits are stored two-wide (4 → 04).
❓
The "??" button
Heard the call but not the zone? Tap ?? — the QSO is logged and still scores its points and country; just look the station up on QRZ afterward and fill the zone in.
📌
Sticky RST & zone
Reports and zones repeat in a run, so both carry over to the next QSO — change them only when they actually differ. (They reset fresh per session.)
🚫
Per-band dupe guard
A station counts once per band. Work a dupe and a box asks before logging it — default No. Same call on another band is a fresh, valid QSO.

Your own zone is set once on the session (for VA2OPS that's 05) and rides along as the sent exchange; the received zone is the only per-QSO entry, and the picker makes it a single tap.

Score

It's scored to the CQ WW rules: QSO points by the worked station's continent (3 points DX, 2 within North America, 0 same-country but still a multiplier), and multipliers counted per band — one for each CQ zone and each country (DXCC entity) you work on that band. Score is points × total multipliers. As you type a call, a small preview shows its point value and whether it's a new zone or new country, so you know what a contact is worth before you log it.

Scored fully offline. Country, continent and default zone come from a bundled cty.dat (the standard AD1C "country file", MIT-licensed) — no internet needed, which matters for EMCOMM and field operating. You can drop an updated cty.dat in your config folder when a new one is published. The score is always recomputed from the whole log, so editing or deleting a QSO never leaves the total out of step.

One-click Cabrillo

When the contest is over, press Export Cabrillo. The logger writes a ready-to-submit Cabrillo 3.0 file to your ~/Documents folder — header lines (callsign, contest, grid, category, claimed score) plus a QSO: line per contact, with frequency, mode, date/time, both calls, both reports and both exchanges in the columns the robots expect. Review the category/score headers, and send it in.

Your ordinary ADIF export is untouched and still on the same bar — so a park-and-contest session gives you a POTA ADIF and a contest Cabrillo from the exact same log.
