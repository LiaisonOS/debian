QtAprsBeacon — APRS beacon + messaging · Touch Mode

Put yourself on the map

APRS is how the ham world shares position, status, and short messages over RF. QtAprsBeacon brings native APRS beaconing to LiaisonOS as a first-class mode — it keys your position onto the local APRS network through your radio and a KISS TNC (Direwolf or a hardware TNC), with nothing to configure beyond your callsign-SSID, path, and symbol.

Instead of beaconing blindly on a fixed timer, QtAprsBeacon is distance-based: it fires an initial beacon when you start, then a fresh one only after you have actually moved a set number of metres (great-circle / Haversine distance). Sitting still uses zero airtime; on the move, your track stays current without hammering the channel.

Key features

📍
Distance-based beaconing
Beacons on movement, not a blind timer — set the minimum distance in metres and QtAprsBeacon fires only when you've actually moved.
💬
APRS messaging
Send and receive APRS text messages to any station, with automatic ACK tracking so you can see when your message was received.
🛰️

👆
Desktop & Touch Mode
A full desktop window or a big-button Touch Mode layout with inline compose and history — plus a lock-screen button for field use.

Paired with LiaisonGPS

The piece that makes this effortless is LiaisonGPS, the LiaisonOS Android companion app. Instead of hunting for a USB GPS dongle, LiaisonGPS turns the phone already in your pocket into your station's GPS source: it reads the phone's location and broadcasts it over Bluetooth as standard NMEA sentences.

LiaisonOS receives that Bluetooth NMEA stream and feeds it to gpsd, the same local GPS service every position-aware app on the system reads. QtAprsBeacon simply asks gpsd for the current fix — so the moment LiaisonGPS is paired and streaming, your beacons carry a live, accurate position. The same fix also drives the Dashboard grid, the Field Logger, and GPS clock-sync.

Why a phone? Modern phones have excellent multi-constellation GNSS receivers, and you always have one with you. LiaisonGPS keeps the GPS off your radio bench and out of your USB ports — one less cable in the field.

How a beacon session works

Pick your position source (GPS from LiaisonGPS, or a manual lat/lon you enter by hand), set your SSID, digipeater path, comment, APRS symbol, and the minimum move distance — then press Start Beaconing. QtAprsBeacon fires an initial position beacon immediately so you appear on the map right away, then watches the GPS fix and beacons again each time you've moved past your distance threshold.

Everything goes out through your KISS TNC over the radio you already have running, so it works fully off-grid — no internet, no APRS-IS gateway required. Anyone monitoring the frequency, or any nearby digipeater and iGate, picks you up.

Messaging, the APRS way

QtAprsBeacon also does APRS messaging. Type a short message to another station and send it; QtAprsBeacon tracks the outgoing message and matches the ackNNNNN reply addressed back to your SSID, so a sent message flips to acknowledged the moment the other end confirms it. Inbound messages addressed to you are surfaced and logged. It is deliberately fire-and-forget — no noisy auto-retry storms on the channel; if you want to resend, you re-send.
