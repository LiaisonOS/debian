Mesh25

Authenticated text over amateur radio — no internet, no infrastructure.

A native C++/Qt messaging station for LiaisonOS: chat, mail, beacons and a live network map that ride plain packet radio, signed with modern Ed25519 keys.

🔑 Ed25519 signed 📻 AX.25 / KISS ⚙ C++ / Qt 📄 MIT open source

What is Mesh25?

Mesh25 is a messaging station for amateur radio. It lets a group of operators chat, send mail, and see who is reachable — entirely over the air on ordinary packet radio, with no internet and no repeater or server in the middle. If you can hear another station, you can message it; if you can't, Mesh25 can route your message through the stations in between.

It is built for emergency communications (EMCOMM): when the cell network and internet are down, a handful of radios running Mesh25 form a small, resilient text network on their own.

See it on the air

A Mesh25 net filmed on four laptops, each with its own radio: stations beacon their positions, check in and chat in the WX and CQ rooms. The conversation was scripted for the camera, but every message you see went over the air on packet radio.

What it does

💬
Chat
An open "CQ" room plus direct one-to-one conversations with any station you hear.
✉️
Store-and-forward mail
Compose messages with a subject and body; they are delivered when the recipient is on the air.
📦
Parking (mailbox)
Leave mail at a station that acts as a mailbox; it is handed over when the recipient shows up.
📶
Beacons
Each station periodically announces its grid location and who it can hear.
🛰️
Relay routing
Messages hop through intermediate stations to reach someone out of direct range (the Pathfinder).
🕸️
Network map
A live "who-hears-whom" graph of the net, placed by grid, with per-station distance — or a real geographic map drawn from offline tilesets, no internet needed.
🗺️
Offline basemap & callsign lookup
Switch the map to a real basemap read straight from LiaisonOS's offline map tilesets — no map server, no internet. Click a station for the operator's name and location from the on-board callsign database. The Tiles button shows which regions are installed and lets you choose one.

How it works

Mesh25 speaks the same radio language as every other packet application — AX.25 frames over a KISS TNC — so it runs with a sound-card TNC (Direwolf), a USB KISS TNC, or a Bluetooth KISS radio. It is a single self-contained program: no extra services, no internet dependency.

Every message can be cryptographically signed with the sender's key, so you can trust that a message really came from the callsign it claims. Mesh25 uses Ed25519, a fast, modern signature scheme. Keys are public — they prove identity, they don't hide the text (amateur radio must stay in the clear). The first time you hear a station, its key is learned automatically; if that key ever changes, you get an alert.

Why "25"? It is a nod to AX.25 — the packet-radio protocol Mesh25 rides on — and it happily echoes the Ed25519 signatures at its core.

Origin & credit

Mesh25 grew out of QtChattervox, a Qt front-end to ChatterVox by Brannon Dorsey — an open-source signed text-messaging protocol for amateur packet radio. ChatterVox proved the concept and its clean, well-documented format is what made this work possible.

Mesh25 is a clean-sheet, all-C++ reimplementation: it contains no ChatterVox source code, uses its own packet format, and upgrades the cryptography from ChatterVox's ECDSA P-192 to Ed25519. It keeps the spirit — authenticated text over amateur radio — and we gratefully acknowledge ChatterVox as the foundation and the inspiration. Thank you, Brannon Dorsey.

Open source

Mesh25 is released under the MIT License and developed in the open at github.com/LiaisonOS/Mesh25. It ships as part of LiaisonOS, the bilingual Debian-based distribution for ham-radio emergency communications. Built with Qt, OpenSSL (Ed25519) and zlib.
